Kitchen renovations in London

Reconfiguring the ground floor around a new kitchen — structural changes, layout design, and full coordination under one director.

What a kitchen renovation actually involves

A kitchen renovation sounds simple until you start. The kitchen is the most technically complex room in any house — water, waste, gas, electrics, ventilation, structural loads, fire safety. Moving a kitchen sink by two metres can mean rerouting drainage under the floor. Adding an island often means moving a soil stack. Knocking through to the dining room means a structural engineer, a steel beam, and a Building Regulations application.

This is why kitchen renovations go wrong more often than extensions. Not because the building work is harder, but because the coordination between trades is more complex and more often left to the builder to figure out on site — which is where the variations and the cost overruns come from.

When you need an architect for a kitchen

You don't always. If you're replacing cabinets, worktops and appliances within the existing footprint — same layout, same walls, same services — a good kitchen designer and a competent builder are all you need.

You need an architect when:

Walls are moving. Removing a load-bearing wall between kitchen and dining room requires structural design, a Building Regulations application, and usually party wall notices to your neighbours. We coordinate all of this.

The layout is changing fundamentally. Moving the kitchen from the back of the house to the middle, or combining three rooms into one, requires a proper floor plan that thinks about services routing, natural light, ventilation, and how the room connects to the rest of the house.

The renovation is part of something larger. If the kitchen is happening alongside a rear extension, a loft conversion, or a whole-house refurbishment, the kitchen design needs to be integrated with the architectural design from the start — not designed separately by a kitchen company and dropped in afterwards.

What it costs

Our design fees for a kitchen renovation range from £2,500 for a single-room reconfiguration with one structural change to £12,000 for a full ground-floor remodelling with multiple wall removals, service rerouting, and coordination with an extension. Fixed fees, agreed in writing.

Build costs for a kitchen renovation in London vary enormously depending on specification — from £15,000 for a basic refit with structural work to £80,000+ for a high-end open-plan ground floor. The architectural fee is typically 8-12% of the build cost.

How we approach a kitchen project

We start with the room, not the cabinets. Where does the light come from? Where does the garden view go? How do you move through the space — from front door to kitchen, from kitchen to garden, from kitchen to dining table? What do you actually cook, how many people eat together, and where do the children do homework?

The layout answers those questions. The cabinets come afterwards — and we're happy to work alongside any kitchen supplier you choose (we have no commercial relationships with cabinet manufacturers).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an architect for a kitchen renovation? +
Not always. If you're replacing cabinets within the same layout, a kitchen designer is enough. You need an architect when walls are moving (structural design + Building Regs), the layout is fundamentally changing (services rerouting), or the kitchen is part of a larger project like an extension.
How much does a kitchen renovation cost in London? +
Our design fees range from £2,500 to £12,000. Build costs vary enormously — from £15,000 for a basic refit with structural work to £80,000+ for a high-end open-plan ground floor. We give you a cost estimate during feasibility.
Do I need planning permission for a kitchen renovation? +
Almost never for internal works. If you're removing walls, you need Building Regulations approval (not planning permission). If the kitchen renovation is part of a rear extension, the extension may need planning — the kitchen itself doesn't.
How long does a kitchen renovation take? +
2-4 months for design and coordination, then 2-4 months on site. Longer if it involves structural changes or is part of a larger project. We sequence the work so you're without a kitchen for as short a time as possible.

Fixed fees in writing. First consultation free.
