Rear extensions in London

The most common brief we take on — and the one where getting the first decisions right matters most.

What a rear extension actually involves

A rear extension replaces the back wall of your house with a new room that opens to the garden. On a typical London Victorian or Edwardian terrace, that means demolishing the narrow return at the back, building out by three to six metres, and creating a single open-plan kitchen, dining and living space where three small rooms used to be.

The result — when it works — is transformative. The ground floor goes from a series of dark, disconnected corridors to one generous room with direct garden access. Natural light reaches the middle of the house for the first time. The kitchen moves from a galley at the back to an island at the centre of the home.

When it doesn't work, the extension feels like a box tacked onto the back of the house. The join between old and new reads as an afterthought. The roof sits at an awkward height. The glazing is too large or too small. The room is either too hot in summer or too cold in winter. These are design decisions, not build problems — and they're made in the first two weeks of the project, not on site.

Do you need planning permission?

Often not. Under permitted development rights, you can extend a detached house by up to four metres to the rear and an attached house by up to three metres, without submitting a planning application — provided the extension is single-storey, doesn't exceed four metres in height, and doesn't cover more than half the garden.

For larger extensions, or for properties in conservation areas, Article 4 directions, or where permitted development rights have been removed, you'll need a householder planning application. This costs £258 in council fees and typically takes eight to thirteen weeks for a decision.

We check your property's specific position — planning history, conservation status, permitted development rights — as part of the free first consultation. You'll know before you spend anything whether your project needs planning permission or not.

What it costs

Our fees for a rear extension typically range from £3,500 for a simple permitted-development scheme through to £18,000 for a full RIBA Stage 1-4 appointment on a larger or more complex project. These are fixed fees, agreed in writing before we start, broken by RIBA stage so you can appoint stage by stage without committing to the whole thing upfront.

Build costs in London for a single-storey rear extension typically run between £2,200 and £3,500 per square metre depending on specification — a typical three-metre rear extension on a Victorian terrace costs £80,000 to £150,000 to build. We give you a rough cost estimate as part of the feasibility study so you can budget before committing to the design stages.

How we work on a rear extension

Every rear extension follows the same six stages, though not every project needs all six:

Stage 1 — Feasibility (2 weeks). We visit the property, review the planning position, and produce three sketch options showing different approaches. Each option comes with a rough cost band so you can compare like for like. This is where you decide whether the project is worth pursuing — and if it is, which direction to take.

Stage 2 — Concept design (4 weeks). We develop your preferred option into a proper design. Plans, sections, elevations, material choices. We walk you through it in a presentation meeting and make adjustments until it's right.

Stage 3 — Planning (4 weeks + 8-13 weeks determination). If planning permission is needed, we prepare and submit the full application — drawings, Design and Access Statement, consultant coordination. We manage the council process, respond to officer queries, and handle any conditions.

Stage 4 — Technical design (6 weeks). The Building Regulations package: construction details, structural coordination with the engineer, specifications, tender documentation. This is the package your contractor builds from. The quality of this package directly determines how many questions arise on site — and how much the build ends up costing versus the tender price.

Stage 5 — Construction (duration of build). We inspect the works on site, respond to contractor queries same-day, administer the building contract, and sign off payment applications. You don't have to interpret drawings or argue with the builder — that's our job.

Stage 6 — Handover. Final inspection, snagging, defects list, practical completion. We don't sign off until it's right.

What can go wrong

Three things cause most problems on rear extension projects, and all three are avoidable:

Party wall disputes. If your extension is within three metres of a neighbour's property (which on a London terrace it almost always is), you need to serve a Party Wall Notice under the 1996 Act. Doing this early and courteously avoids disputes. Doing it late or badly causes delays of months. We draft and serve the notices as part of the service.

Underspecified tender documents. If the drawings your contractor prices from are vague, the price is a guess. Guesses become variations. Variations become disputes. Our Stage 4 package is designed to minimise this — every junction detailed, every specification written, every structural element coordinated with the engineer before it reaches the contractor.

Poor design decisions at concept stage. The proportions of the opening to the garden. The relationship between old roof and new. Whether the extension reads as part of the house or as an addition. These decisions happen in weeks one and two and they determine whether the finished project adds value to your home or detracts from it. This is why we show you three options, not one — so the decision is yours, informed and compared.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission for a rear extension? +
Often not. Many rear extensions fall under permitted development — up to 3m depth on an attached house, 4m on a detached one, single-storey, under 4m height. We check your specific property's position (planning history, conservation status, Article 4 directions) as part of the free first consultation. You'll know before you spend anything.
How much does a rear extension cost in London? +
Our design fees range from £3,500 to £18,000 depending on complexity and how many RIBA stages you appoint us for. Build costs in London typically run £2,200-£3,500 per square metre — a typical 3m Victorian terrace extension costs £80,000-£150,000 to build. We give you a cost estimate during feasibility.
How long does a rear extension take from start to finish? +
Typically 3-5 months for design and planning consent, 4-8 weeks for the technical package, and 3-5 months on site. Total: 9-14 months from first meeting to moving back in. We give you a written programme at the start so you can plan around it.
Will a rear extension add value to my house? +
A well-designed rear extension typically adds 15-25% to a London property's value — significantly more than the cost of the works. A poorly designed one can reduce value. The difference is in the design decisions made at concept stage, which is why we show you three options and explain the trade-offs before you commit.
What is the difference between permitted development and planning permission? +
Permitted development is a set of pre-approved rights that let you build certain extensions without applying to the council. If your extension falls within the rules (size, height, position, materials), you don't need permission — you just build. If it exceeds the rules, or if your property's PD rights have been removed, you need a householder planning application (£258 fee, 8-13 weeks). We check which route applies to your property before any design work begins.
